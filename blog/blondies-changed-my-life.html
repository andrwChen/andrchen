A Caramel Blondie Changed My Life

I've been deathly allergic to two things for two decades of my life: eggs and peanuts. If I eat either of them, I will experience any of the following:

  • Itchy, red hives and welts covering my entire body
  • A swollen tongue and throat, which makes it hard to breathe
  • Vomiting. Lots of it.
  • An impending sense of doom (yes, this is an actual medical symptom)
  • Death from anaphylactic shock (unlikely if I have an EpiPen with me, but possible)

I've experienced every single thing in the list above (except the last one) several times. I've been hospitalized several times as a child after eating at restaurants that didn't properly prepare my food. But it's not necessarily the symptoms of having an allergic reaction that make it so hard to live with.

As an avid lover of food, I can describe no greater feeling of utter sadness than being surrounded by delicious food and not being able to eat any of it. Every birthday means staring at a cake you'll never get to have a single bite of. You'd find me eating ice cream or some shitty vegan cake instead. Every potluck is pure torture. Assuming there's even something that doesn't contain eggs at the gathering (sometimes there's nothing), cross-contamination from people inadvertently mixing foods together whilst serving themselves means I'd have to just sit with an empty plate and watch others eat. Food brings people together; with an allergy, it can push you apart. Unbeknownst to many, it is truly a miserable life.

When I was a young teenager, my doctor sent me to an allergist to test whether I was eligible for a food-tolerance program. These programs are intended to routinely expose you to miniscule amounts of the food that you're allergic to. With time, you may develop tolerance and "outgrow" your food allergy. After a couple of tests, however, it became abundantly clear that I would not be able to participate. My allergen sensitivity readings were so high that exposing me to any amount of eggs or peanuts would be unsafe. I was extremely disappointed but not surprised.

Cut to 20-year-old me. I am working a 9-5 at Harvard Law School as a Technical Support Services person (IT job). It's not technically a 9-5 during the shift I'm working at on - I'm working the 2:30-10:30 PM shift. It's 8:30 PM, the office is dead empty and quiet, and there's three caramel blondies sitting on the kitchen table.

They smell so fucking good. They look absolutely delicious. I am beyond livid. This is precisely the kind of shit I've gone through countless times before, but it still stings to be in the presence of food I cannot have. I know what will happen if I eat the blondie (see list above). I know it'd be incredibly stupid to eat something that is known to contain eggs, especially considering that there is no one around to save my life if I had an allergic reaction. Sure, I had my EpiPen. But even if I used it, it's still necessary to call 911 because an allergic reaction can always come back.

After about 10 minutes of hesitant deliberation, I cave. I take the smallest little nibble (literally the size of a grain of rice) out of one of the blondies. I know that, if the blondies contain eggs, I will immediately be able to tell because my body is so sensitive. But I feel nothing. I wait 15 minutes. Still nothing. Under normal circumstances, my tongue and throat would be swollen and itchy by now.

So I take another bite, about 1 gram this time. I wait another 15 minutes. Still nothing. At this point, I take a big step and eat half of the caramel blondie. I immediately regret it. The back of my mind is screaming at me, telling me how stupid I was for doing this, how if I get a reaction now, the ambulance ride is going to be so embarrassing to explain. But 30 minutes later, the only thing I feel is fear mixed with excitement - not the urge to scratch hundreds of red welts around my body, vomit my dinner, or take a Benadryl.

The next day, I entered work elated yet scared. What if my fears that the blondies were eggless were confirmed? I'd feel like such an idiot then. I asked the woman who baked the blondies for the ingredients list. When she told me with an incredulous smile that they did contain eggs, I nearly jumped with joy. I couldn't believe my ears. I was free. I read over the Trader Joe's box mix she used and my heart nearly sank when I found it didn't contain eggs, but was then quickly lifted again when I realized that the recipe called for adding "1 cracked egg" to the mix.

I immediately called my mom, imploring her to call my doctor and get blood work done so we could see where I stand in the allergy scene. I then ate so many good baked-egg goods: chocolate-chip cookies, Hot Pockets, pizza, pumpkin bread, Hawaiian cookies, roast-beef sandwiches, Italian subs, and so many more. Prior to my allergen revelation, I had never been able to eat so many breads and baked sweets because they always contained eggs - I might as well have had celiac disease.

My quality of life has significantly improved. I'm still in disbelief that the way I found out I could tolerate eggs in baked goods (that's an important distinction from a sunny-side up egg, but I digress) was because someone in the office decided to bring some baked blondies to work for someone else's birthday and share them.

I'm unsure whether I can claim that I would have never found out about outgrowing my egg allergy until the day I die. I think I would find out eventually somehow. But those blondies were fucking delicious, and opened my life up to so much. That's really all I have to say.

Disclaimer:

If you have a food allergy, DO NOT DO THIS!!! You could die. You may not be as lucky as me, and you should only ever test your tolerance to an allergen in the presence of medical professionals in a controlled environment. If you do not have a food allergy, do not use my stupid actions to undermine the seriousness of people who have food allergies.
